The exam changed in 2022. Most prep didn't.

Today's NCMHCE hands you the diagnosis and asks what you'd do: assess, plan, intervene and stay in scope. It's a test of clinical decision-making, not a diagnosis quiz. PassReady Prep cases mirror that. Every decision is scored from −2 to +3, so your practice shows how close a near miss was, and when a confident choice would have harmed the client.

−2Harmful or out of scope, the costliest kind of mistake

Questions candidates ask

NCMHCE and NCE prep, answered

Straight answers on the exams, the study plan, and access extensions.

What is the NCMHCE?

The National Clinical Mental Health Counseling Examination is NBCC’s clinical licensure exam, required by many state boards for the LPC or LMHC license. The current format, introduced in 2022, presents 11 case studies (10 scored and 1 unscored pretest case), each with 9 to 15 multiple-choice questions that follow a client from intake through treatment. You have 225 minutes plus a scheduled 15-minute break.

What is the difference between the NCE and the NCMHCE?

The NCE is a 200-question multiple-choice exam on general counseling knowledge across six content domains; from July 1, 2027 it moves to 170 items with three answer options. The NCMHCE is a clinical exam built from case simulations that score your decisions about assessment, diagnosis, treatment and ethics. Which one you take depends on your state board; some states require one, some accept either, and some require both. PassReady Prep covers both exams under one account, each with its own free trial and plans.

How is the NCMHCE scored, and what is a passing score?

NBCC reports a scaled score and sets the passing standard; it does not publish a fixed number of questions you must get right, so confirm the current details in your candidate handbook. PassReady Prep’s readiness score is our own estimate: it weights your accuracy by how heavily each domain counts on the exam, and every decision in a practice case is scored from −2 to +3 so you can see how close a near miss was.

How long should I study for the NCMHCE?

Most candidates do well with 8 to 12 weeks of one practice case a day plus focused drills on their weakest domain. Rather than counting weeks, watch two markers: a readiness score that holds above 60%, and two full timed mock exams in the final two weeks. Retakers usually need less time and a sharper focus on the domain that failed them.
