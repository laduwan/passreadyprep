Overpromising misrepresents what the counselor actually believes and could undermine trust if progress is slow.
B. What do you think it would mean for you if this didn't end up working out?
Deflecting a direct question with a question avoids being genuine with a client who asked for honesty.

This withholds the counselor's honest, informed view, which she does in fact have.

At his first session, a 26-year-old graduate student from Ghana whose name is Kwabena says, "Most people here just call me Kevin. It's easier." Which counselor response BEST conveys respect?
A. I'm happy to use whichever you prefer. If Kwabena, will you help me say it? ✔ KEY
This leaves the choice with him and shows willingness to learn his name, conveying respect without pressure.
B. Kevin is fine with me, then. It will keep things simple for both of us here.
Accepting the anglicized name for convenience skips asking what he prefers and centers ease over his identity.
C. I'll call you Kwabena, because it's important to honor your true heritage.
Insisting on his birth name overrides his choice; respect means honoring his preference, whatever it is.
D. Do you feel people here haven't respected your culture since you arrived?
Jumping to a probing question about discrimination presumes a problem before learning what he wants to be called.
Rationale: Respect includes honoring how clients wish to be addressed. Rather than defaulting to convenience or imposing a choice, the counselor asks for his preference and offers to learn correct pronunciation. Small acts like this signal that the client's identity matters and that he directs how it is represented.
