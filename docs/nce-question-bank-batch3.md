> Tarasoff II held a duty to use reasonable care to protect the victim, which warning may or may not satisfy.

### 191. nce-s-cor-039 · Counseling and Helping Relationships · unconditional positive regard · medium · In review

A 26-year-old client in outpatient treatment for opioid use returns after relapsing following 90 days of abstinence. He looks down and says, "You must be so disappointed in me." Which counselor response BEST demonstrates unconditional positive regard?

**A. [ ]** I'm a bit disappointed, but I know you can do better than this next time.
> This makes the counselor's regard contingent on his behavior, which is the opposite of unconditional positive regard.

**B. [ ]** Relapse is just part of recovery, so it's really not a big deal at all.
> This minimizes a serious event and offers reassurance rather than acceptance of the person and his feelings.

**C. [✔]** My regard for you hasn't changed. I'm glad you came back to talk about it.
> This separates his worth from the relapse and welcomes him back, communicating acceptance that does not depend on his behavior.

**D. [ ]** Let's go over what you did wrong so this does not happen to you again.

### 194. nce-s-cor-042 · Counseling and Helping Relationships · congruence and genuineness · hard · In review

A 31-year-old client who says coworkers avoid her asks, "Be honest. Do you find me annoying? Everyone else does." In their sessions, the counselor has felt irritated when the client cuts her off mid-sentence. Which response BEST demonstrates congruence in the client's service?

**A. [ ]** Not at all. I truly enjoy every single minute of our time together in here.
> This reassurance contradicts the counselor's actual experience, so it is incongruent and withholds useful feedback.

**B. [ ]** Honestly, yes. You can be pretty exhausting for most people to be around.
> This is a global, hurtful judgment rather than a specific, caring observation; genuineness is not the same as bluntness.

**C. [✔]** At times I feel cut off when you jump in. I wonder if others feel that too.
> This honestly shares a specific, behavior-based reaction in the here and now and links it to the client's concern, which is genuine and therapeutic.

**D. [ ]** What makes you want to ask me that question at this point in our work?
> Exploring the question can be useful, but used here it sidesteps a direct, sincere request for honest feedback.

**Rationale:** Congruence means the counselor's responses match inner experience, but it is expressed with care and for the client's benefit. Sharing a specific, behavioral reaction in the moment (immediacy) gives the client accurate interpersonal feedback she may not get elsewhere. False reassurance is incongruent, and blunt global judgments are not genuineness but a failure of respect.
