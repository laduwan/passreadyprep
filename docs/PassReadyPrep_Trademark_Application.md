## 1. The mark

| Field | Value |
|---|---|
| Mark type | **Standard character mark** |
| Mark | **PASSREADY PREP** |
| Literal element | PASSREADY PREP |

Why standard characters: it protects the words themselves in any font, size,
or color — the broadest protection, and it covers both spellings you actually
use ("PassReady Prep" in the App Store, "PassReadyPrep" on the site, since
case and spacing are not distinguishing in a standard character mark... note:
spacing *is* literal, so file it as two words, **PASSREADY PREP**, matching
your App Store name; USPTO treats "PASSREADYPREP" vs "PASSREADY PREP" as
essentially the same mark for conflict purposes, but file the form you lead
with publicly).

A logo (design mark) filing can come later as a second application once the
wordmark (`docs/ads/passready-logo-wordmark-1200x300.png`) is final. Words
first — that's where the value is.

**Expect a disclaimer requirement:** the examiner will almost certainly
require you to disclaim the exclusive right to "PREP" apart from the mark as
a whole, because it is descriptive of exam-prep services. This is normal,
costs nothing, and does not weaken the registration. If asked, respond with:
*"No claim is made to the exclusive right to use 'PREP' apart from the mark
as shown."*

## 3. Goods and services — two classes

Use the **pre-approved descriptions from the Trademark ID Manual**
(https://idm-tmng.uspto.gov) exactly as below. Picking from the ID Manual
avoids the $200/class free-form surcharge. Search the ID Manual for the
description text; select the matching entry rather than typing your own.

### Class 41 — the web service (core class)

> Educational services, namely, providing on-line courses of instruction,
> practice tests, and study guides in the field of professional licensure
> examination preparation for mental health counselors

(ID Manual building blocks: "Educational services, namely, providing
{indicate type} in the field of {indicate subject}" and "Providing on-line
practice examinations". If the exact combined wording above isn't offered
as one entry, select the closest ID Manual entries for (a) on-line courses
of instruction, (b) practice tests / practice examinations, and (c) study
guides, each completed with "in the field of professional licensure
examination preparation for mental health counselors".)

### Class 9 — the downloadable app

> Downloadable mobile applications for professional licensure examination
> preparation in the field of mental health counseling

(ID Manual pattern: "Downloadable mobile applications for {specify
function}".)

### Skipped on purpose

- **Class 42** (non-downloadable SaaS) — your Class 41 educational-services
  description already covers the web platform as consumers encounter it;
  adding 42 mostly adds $350 without adding much scope for an ed-prep brand.
- Never put **NCMHCE** or **NCE** in your identification or your mark —
  NCMHCE is NBCC's registered mark. Describing the *field* generically
  ("mental health counseling licensure examination preparation") is correct
  and safe.

## 4. Filing basis and dates

Both classes should be **Section 1(a) — use in commerce**, because the site
is live and selling plans and the iOS app is released. For each class you
must give two dates and one specimen:

| Field | Class 41 (web service) | Class 9 (app) |
|---|---|---|
| Date of first use anywhere | `[FILL IN — the day the PassReady Prep brand was first used publicly, e.g. site launch]` | `[FILL IN — the day the app first became available]` |
| Date of first use in commerce | `[FILL IN — first sale/paying customer under the brand, or first public availability of the service]` | `[FILL IN — App Store release date, US storefront]` |

Rules of thumb:
- "Anywhere" date is the same as or earlier than the "in commerce" date.
- Estimate conservatively (later is safer than earlier); the dates must be
  accurate "to the best of your knowledge" and are sworn under penalty of
  perjury.
- If the iOS app is **not yet live** on the App Store at filing time, file
  Class 9 under **Section 1(b) — intent to use** instead; you'll file a
  Statement of Use later ($150/class extra, after launch). Mixed-basis
  applications (1(a) for one class, 1(b) for the other) are fine.

## 8. Filing steps

1. Create/sign in to a USPTO.gov account (verified identity required for
   filing): https://account.uspto.gov
2. Run the clearance searches in §0.
3. Capture and save the two specimens (§5).
4. Start the application at https://www.uspto.gov/trademarks/apply →
   "Apply for a trademark" (Trademark Center).
5. Enter the owner, mark, and the two ID-Manual classes exactly as above;
   attach specimens; enter the first-use dates.
6. Pay $700, sign, submit. Save the serial number from the confirmation.
7. Calendar the follow-ups below.

## 9. What happens after filing

- **~5–8 months:** an examining attorney reviews it. Expect at most a minor
  office action (the "PREP" disclaimer); respond within **3 months** of any
  office action (a 3-month extension costs $125).
- **Publication:** 30-day opposition window in the Official Gazette.
- **Registration:** certificate issues a few weeks after publication if
  unopposed. Then you may use **®** (until then, keep using ™, which you
  can use right now at no cost).
- **Maintenance:** Section 8 declaration between years 5–6; Section 8+9
  renewal at year 10. Calendar both.
- Add to the site footer once filed: "PassReady Prep™ is a trademark of
  GA Integrated Therapeutic Perspectives LLC."

## 10. The method marks — THE OWNERSHIP METHOD and RUNG WISE

Your study guides coin two method names: **the Ownership Method** (the NCE
study approach, sold as "The NCE Ownership Method") and **Rung Wise** (the
NCMHCE approach built on the Priority Ladder). Both can be registered as
separate marks later; neither should go in this first filing.

### THE OWNERSHIP METHOD — file second

- **Fix the name before filing:** the product is currently sold as "The NCE
  Ownership Method," but **NCE is NBCC's trademark** — another party's mark
  cannot sit inside yours. File **THE OWNERSHIP METHOD** and shift the
  product display name toward "The Ownership Method™ (NCE study guide)".
- **Basis:** Section 1(a) — it is already sold through Stripe and listed on
  the checkout and guarantee pages, which is genuine use in commerce.
- **Class 9:** "Downloadable electronic publications in the nature of study
  guides in the field of professional licensure examination preparation for
  mental health counselors." Add Class 41 only if it becomes a course.
- **Expect disclaimers** of "THE" and "METHOD" — routine, harmless.
- **Specimen:** the checkout/sales page showing the name next to the buy
  button, with URL and date.

### RUNG WISE — file when it's actually in use

- A coined term — inherently distinctive, the strongest kind of mark.
  Rung Wise™ is now branded into the NCMHCE Complete Study Guide (the
  framework introduction in Part 1, the index, the Amazon listing, and the
  back-matter trademark notice). Use in commerce begins when the branded
  edition is actually **sold** — once the updated ebook is live on Amazon /
  the sales page, you can file under 1(a); before that, file intent-to-use
  (extra $150 Statement of Use fee after launch).
- **Class 9** (downloadable study guide) and/or **41** (educational
  services), same field wording as above.

### PRIORITY LADDER — don't register

Your own content uses it generically and in lowercase as the name of the
concept ("the priority ladder's second rung"), which both weakens it as a
mark and would undercut a filing. Keep it as framework vocabulary under the
Rung Wise brand.

### Cost and sequence

1. **Now:** PASSREADY PREP, two classes — $700 (§1–8 above). Start using ™
   on "The Ownership Method™" and introduce "Rung Wise™" in the product.
2. **Next:** THE OWNERSHIP METHOD, Class 9 — $350.
3. **Later:** RUNG WISE, Class 9 and/or 41 — $350–700 (+$150 if filed
   intent-to-use).

Copyright already protects the guides' *text* automatically; registering
the copyright (copyright.gov, ~$45–65 per work) adds the right to statutory
damages and is worth doing for each finished guide. Trademark protects only
the names as brands.
