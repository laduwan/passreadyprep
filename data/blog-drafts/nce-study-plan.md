Most people sitting for the National Counselor Examination are finishing a graduate program, starting a first counseling job, or both. A study plan has to fit into that life. The plan below spreads preparation across six weeks and follows the way NBCC builds the exam: by domain, with the heaviest-weighted areas getting the most time. Treat it as a scaffold. Stretch it, compress it, or swap weeks around to fit your schedule and your test date.

## Before week one: get oriented

Spend an hour on logistics first. Read NBCC's NCE Candidate Handbook so you know exactly what you are training for. If you test before July 1, 2027, the exam has 200 multiple-choice questions with four options each. 160 are scored and 40 are unscored field-test items you cannot tell apart from the rest. You have 225 minutes to answer, with a scheduled break after question 100. You can test at a Pearson VUE test center or online through Pearson's OnVUE remote proctoring. If your date is on or after July 1, 2027, read the 2027 exam specifications instead, since the format and domains change.

Also check with your state licensing board. Each board decides which exam it uses for licensure and when you are eligible to sit for it.

Then take a short mixed practice set cold. Do not review first. Note which domains feel shaky. That is your starting map.

## Week 1: Counseling skills and theories

Counseling Skills and Interventions is the largest domain, with 48 scored items (30%), so start here. Build a one-page sheet of theory "signatures": the key figure, view of human nature, goal of counseling, and two or three signature techniques for each approach. Cover psychodynamic, Adlerian, person-centered, existential, gestalt, behavioral, CBT, REBT, reality therapy, solution-focused, narrative, and feminist approaches, plus motivational interviewing and DBT.

Add family systems (Bowen, structural, strategic) and crisis intervention models. Then review microskills as a sequence: attending, paraphrasing, reflecting feeling, summarizing, and finally influencing skills like confrontation and immediacy.

**This week's practice:** short daily sets on theory recognition, plus scenario questions that ask for the best counselor response.

## Week 2: Areas of clinical focus

Areas of Clinical Focus is nearly as large, with 47 scored items (29%). This domain covers the client concerns you will work with: depressive, bipolar, anxiety, trauma-related, substance use, and other disorders, along with grief and loss, crisis response, intimate partner violence, child abuse and neglect, career concerns, couples and family issues, life transitions, and work with specific populations.

For each topic, aim for three things: how it presents in a short vignette, the feature that distinguishes it from look-alikes, and what a counselor would reasonably do first. Breadth matters more than depth here.

**This week's practice:** mixed clinical-focus questions so you practice identifying the concern without being told the topic.

## Week 3: Human development, career, and groups

These CACREP content areas show up throughout the exam. Review Erikson, Piaget, and Kohlberg, plus attachment theory. For career development, know Holland's types, Super's life-span approach, and Krumboltz's social learning view well enough to recognize them in a scenario. For groups, study the stages of group development, therapeutic factors, leadership skills, and how confidentiality works differently in group settings.

**This week's practice:** flashcards for stage theories, and scenario questions about group dynamics.

## Week 4: Intake, assessment, and diagnosis

This domain has 19 scored items (12%), and it includes the measurement concepts many candidates find hardest. Cover the intake interview, mental status exam, risk assessment, and DSM-5-TR structure. Then work through testing fundamentals: reliability and validity types, norm- versus criterion-referenced tests, standard scores and percentiles, the normal curve, and standard error of measurement. Finish with common screening instruments and culturally fair assessment.

**This week's practice:** a few measurement questions every day. Short, frequent repetition works better than a single long session for this material.

## Week 5: Ethics, treatment planning, and core attributes

Professional Practice and Ethics has 19 scored items (12%). Review informed consent, confidentiality and its limits, duty to warn and protect, mandated reporting, multiple relationships, scope of competence, record keeping, and ethical decision-making models.

Treatment Planning (14 items, 9%) covers measurable goals, level of care, evidence-based treatment selection, progress monitoring, referral, and discharge planning, along with research design and program evaluation basics. Core Counseling Attributes (13 items, 8%) asks about empathy, congruence, unconditional positive regard, the therapeutic alliance, cultural humility, and countertransference.

**This week's practice:** ethics scenarios where two options seem reasonable. Ask which one protects the client and stays within your role.

## Week 6: Timed practice and targeted review

Shift from learning to rehearsing. Take at least one full-length timed exam that mirrors the real one: 200 questions, 225 minutes, with a break after question 100. Practice clearing flagged items before the break, since you cannot return to the first half afterward. [NCE practice questions and mock exams](/nce.html) from PassReady Prep are weighted to the six domains and track your progress by domain, which makes it easier to see where to spend your last few days.

Then focus on your two or three weakest domains. In the final two days, stop adding new material, review your one-page sheets, and confirm logistics. At a test center you need two original, unexpired IDs: a government-issued primary ID with your name, photo, and signature, and a secondary ID with your name and signature. The name must exactly match your registration.

## Making the plan work for you

- **Short beats long.** Forty focused minutes most days beats one exhausting weekend.
- **Retrieve, don't reread.** Questions, flashcards, and explaining ideas out loud build stronger memory than highlighting.
- **Keep a miss log by domain.** Patterns matter more than any single score.
- **Take care of yourself.** Sleep and rest are part of preparation, not a break from it.

## Final thoughts

Six weeks of steady, organized study can turn a semester's worth of coursework into confident, usable knowledge. Start with the largest domains, cover the rest systematically, and finish with realistic timed practice. If life interrupts, shift the plan rather than abandoning it.
