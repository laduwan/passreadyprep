The NCE content outline is the closest thing you have to a map of the exam. It tells you what NBCC tests and how much each area counts. It can also be confusing, because the exam is described in two ways at once: six weighted domains and eight CACREP content areas. This post explains both, how they relate, and how to turn the outline into a practical study plan.

## Start with the format

If you test before July 1, 2027, the National Counselor Examination has 200 multiple-choice questions with four options each. 160 are scored and 40 are unscored field-test items. The content outline describes how those 160 scored items are distributed.

## The six domains

NBCC's 2023 NCE Content Outline organizes the scored items into six domains based on the work counselors actually do. Each domain has a fixed number of items:

| Domain | Scored items | Share |
|---|---|---|
| Counseling Skills and Interventions | 48 | 30% |
| Areas of Clinical Focus | 47 | 29% |
| Professional Practice and Ethics | 19 | 12% |
| Intake, Assessment, and Diagnosis | 19 | 12% |
| Treatment Planning | 14 | 9% |
| Core Counseling Attributes | 13 | 8% |

Here is what each one covers in practical terms.

### Counseling Skills and Interventions (30%)

The largest domain. Expect counseling theories and their signature techniques, from psychodynamic and Adlerian to CBT, REBT, solution-focused, narrative, and motivational interviewing. It also includes family systems approaches, group stages and leadership, basic and advanced microskills, crisis intervention, and multicultural counseling competencies.

### Areas of Clinical Focus (29%)

The concerns clients bring to counseling. This includes depressive, bipolar, anxiety, trauma-related, and substance use disorders, along with grief and loss, intimate partner violence, child abuse and neglect, career concerns, couples and family issues, life transitions, chronic illness, and the needs of specific populations. Breadth matters here.

### Professional Practice and Ethics (12%)

Informed consent, confidentiality and its limits, duty to warn and protect, mandated reporting, multiple relationships, scope of competence, record keeping, ethical decision-making models, supervision, and professional identity.

### Intake, Assessment, and Diagnosis (12%)

The intake interview, mental status exam, risk assessment, and DSM-5-TR structure, plus the measurement concepts many candidates find hardest: reliability, validity, standard scores, percentiles, and standard error of measurement.

### Treatment Planning (9%)

Measurable goals, matching level of care to need, evidence-based treatment selection, progress monitoring, referral, and discharge planning. Research design and program evaluation concepts often connect here as well.

### Core Counseling Attributes (8%)

Empathy, unconditional positive regard, congruence, the therapeutic alliance, cultural humility, and counselor self-awareness, including countertransference.

## The eight CACREP areas

NBCC also notes that NCE items reflect the eight original CACREP content areas:

1. Professional Counseling Orientation and Ethical Practice
2. Social and Cultural Diversity
3. Human Growth and Development
4. Career Development
5. Counseling and Helping Relationships
6. Group Counseling and Group Work
7. Assessment and Testing
8. Research and Program Evaluation

These will look familiar, because they likely shaped your graduate coursework. The key point is that NBCC publishes no item counts per CACREP area. The domains carry the weights. The CACREP areas describe the knowledge the questions draw on.

## How the two fit together

Think of the domains as the questions NBCC asks, and the CACREP areas as the knowledge you need to answer them. A single question can sit in one domain while drawing on several CACREP areas.

For example, a question about choosing an intervention for a teenage client from an immigrant family sits in Counseling Skills and Interventions. To answer it well, you may need Human Growth and Development (adolescence), Social and Cultural Diversity (acculturation), and Counseling and Helping Relationships (theory and technique). Likewise, a question about interpreting a standard score sits in Intake, Assessment, and Diagnosis but draws directly on Assessment and Testing.

This is why studying only by course topic can leave gaps. You may know developmental theory cold but miss the question because you have never practiced applying it to a counseling decision.

## Turning the outline into a study plan

- **Weight your time by domain.** The two largest domains account for 95 of 160 scored items. Give them the most time, but do not skip the smaller ones.
- **Use CACREP areas as a coverage checklist.** Make sure each of the eight areas gets at least one focused review, especially Career Development and Research and Program Evaluation, which are easy to neglect.
- **Practice mixed questions.** Real items do not announce their domain. Mixed practice trains you to identify what a question is testing.
- **Track progress by domain.** Your score report gives general feedback by domain, so measuring your practice the same way keeps you aligned. [NCE practice questions and mock exams](/nce.html) from PassReady Prep are weighted to the six domains and show your progress in each.

## What changes in 2027

From July 1, 2027, the NCE moves to 170 questions with three options each, 140 scored and 30 field-test, with 225 minutes and an optional 15-minute break halfway. The six domains are replaced by new ones: Professional Development and Counselor Self-Awareness; Intake and Assessment; Treatment Planning and Continuity of Care; Provision of Counseling Interventions; Indirect Client Care; and Legal and Ethical Compliance. If your test date falls after the change, study from the 2027 exam specifications.

Which exam you need, and when you are eligible, is decided by your state licensing board, so confirm both with your board before you plan.

## Final thoughts

The content outline turns a broad, intimidating exam into a defined set of domains with known weights. Use the domains to decide where to spend your time, the CACREP areas to make sure nothing is missing, and mixed practice to connect the two.
